# Search methodology

## Define the scope

Extract the main concept, actors or institutions, time span, historical and modern place names, and relevant disciplines. Where available, borrow period-specific vocabulary, translations and transliterations from scholarly titles, abstracts or catalog subject headings. Use modern place names where they help retrieve records and evaluate geographical fit against historical boundaries.

For 16th-century horse naming in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, start with combinations such as `"horse names" "Grand Duchy of Lithuania"`, `"imiona koni" "Wielkie Księstwo Litewskie"` and `"Pferdenamen" "Großfürstentum Litauen"`. Try hipponyms, equine onomastics, or equivalents as retrieval terms. Broader zoonymy can supply methods. Check whether naming vocabulary refers to individual animals, species, breeds or types before ranking the work. Horse breeding or cavalry history is contextual only if it contributes something specific to the naming question. Use Lithuanian or Latin terms if they help locate an English, Polish or German work. Verify the work's language independently of the query, catalog interface, title, or translated abstract.

Use a few high-value combinations per accepted publication language across the search, reusing shared names and terms. Add variants when a query misses plausible coverage or an early record yields better vocabulary. Keep adjacent-concept queries separate from core-topic queries so they do not displace the original question.

## Run the passes

1. **International initial discovery.** Select suitable broad indexes based on subject, publication languages, and access. Start with the original concept and essential variants. OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar can surface candidates, and Crossref can confirm deposited metadata. CORE and DOAJ are useful for open literature within their coverage.
2. **International expansion and targeting.** Use the early results to choose historical variants and sources with complementary coverage, such as JSTOR, Project MUSE or Historical Abstracts. Use a known candidate's DOI or title to verify a record. A publisher visit for verification does not constitute a publisher database search.
3. **National and regional discovery.** After international discovery, choose sources by historical geography, cultural relationships, subject coverage and publication languages. In Quick mode, run at least one focused regional query. In Balanced and Deep, broaden coverage where it is likely to help. If a regional interface is blocked or unavailable, try an alternative regional source or a permitted domain-scoped query. If every feasible route is unavailable, record the routes attempted and the remaining coverage gap.
4. **Verification and citation exploration.** Deduplicate at discovery and again before delivery. Verify likely inclusions, including regional results. Balanced may follow a few promising references or citing papers. Deep should explore backward and forward citations where available and use the resulting terms and references to check geographical, temporal and language gaps. Verify newly found candidates by the same rules.

## Budget by depth

Use these starting ranges internally. They are targets for tool-mediated searches, not required quotas or additional intake questions. Count a batch by the distinct queries it issues. The verification column counts distinct candidates, each of which may require several record checks. The citation column counts seed works.

| Depth | Initial queries | Expanded queries | Targeted international queries | Regional queries | Candidates to verify | Citation seeds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Quick | 2–4 | 0–2 | 0–2 | 1–2 | 5–12 | 0 |
| Balanced | 3–6 | 2–5 | 2–4 | 2–5 | 10–20 | 1–3 when useful |
| Deep | 5–9 | 5–10 | 4–8 | 4–8 | 15–35 | 3–6 where accessible |

Raise or redistribute ranges for additional publication languages, sparse topics, or a larger requested bibliography when useful. Protect the regional budget and allow enough verification for every included item. Use normalized identifiers and previously opened records to avoid repeated searches. Stop expanding after the required passes when the budget is spent, candidate coverage is adequate, or new queries are yielding duplicates or weak matches. Report material coverage gaps. Describe an apparent gap as a finding of this search, with its scope and limitations.
