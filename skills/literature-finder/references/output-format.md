# Output format and verification

## Verify and rank

- Match the candidate against a publisher, journal, institutional repository, national library catalog or reliable bibliographic index. Confirm authors, original title, year, venue or publisher, volume, issue, page range or article number when applicable, publication type and language. Use an independent record where available. Resolve conflicts about the cited version before inclusion. Withhold a candidate if its identity, relevance or allowed publication language cannot be established.
- Confirm language through the work itself, a reliable edition-specific language field or equivalent direct evidence. A translated abstract or an index filter alone can be misleading. For a multilingual edition with substantive text in an accepted language, identify the languages and the version or portion that qualifies. An optional lead outside the verified bibliography may be labeled **Publication language unverified** if it is especially valuable.
- Confirm a DOI against the record it identifies. Resolve it where practical and check the title, authors and version. When a matching authoritative record confirms the DOI but the host cannot resolve it, state that resolution could not be checked and include a working stable record when available. If the DOI is missing or cannot be matched, use a stable publication URL. Show `—` if neither is available. Cite the verification source even when it lacks a stable per-item link. Deduplicate by normalized DOI, then by normalized title, authors, year and edition or version. Prefer the verified version of record over a corresponding preprint or repost. Keep distinct editions or translations only when independently useful.
- Check Crossmark, publisher notices or reliable index metadata for significant corrections or retractions when available. Use corrected details where supported. Exclude retracted work from the bibliography unless the research explicitly calls for it, and label any such inclusion.
- Prioritize peer-reviewed journal articles, scholarly chapters, then monographs or edited volumes among comparably relevant works. Use conference papers, preprints, dissertations and working papers when they add value. Include scholarly critical editions when relevant. Treat an unedited primary object or facsimile as a source lead outside the scholarly bibliography. Label the publication type and distinguish confirmed peer review from **Peer review unverified**. A sharing-platform upload or a current policy without evidence that it applied to the cited issue does not establish peer review.
- Mark **Open Access** when a legitimate free full-text version is confirmed and link to it where possible. If it is a repository manuscript, identify the version when known. This label describes verified reading access. Infer reuse rights only from an explicit license or rights statement. Use **Paywalled / Restricted** for a confirmed restriction when no legitimate free version is found, and **Access Unknown** when availability is unclear. A freely visible abstract or metadata record does not establish open full-text access.

## Annotated bibliography

Use the following numbered format, grouping **Directly relevant literature** and **Related or contextual literature** separately. Keep numbering continuous and indicate if a group has no verified matches. Localize headings, fields, and labels to the conversation language.

### 1. Author — Original Publication Title (Year)

- **Publication** — Journal or publisher, volume, issue and pages or article number where applicable.
- **Type** — Publication type and peer-review status when relevant.
- **Language** — Verified publication language.
- **Access** — Access label and legitimate free copy where applicable.
- **Relevance** — Direct or contextual. For contextual results, identify the specific mismatch in concept, period, geography or method.
- **DOI / URL** — Verified DOI link, stable publication URL, or `—`.
- **Three-sentence summary** — In the conversation language, describe the topic, evidence or method where stated, and contribution or relevance. Base all three sentences on an actual abstract or sufficient publication content. When neither is available, use **Abstract unavailable.** in the conversation language. Never infer a summary from the title or a search snippet.
- **Discovery source** — The service through which the candidate was actually found.
- **Publication host** — Official record or text host, when verified.
- **Verification** — Link the records used to check the citation and, where useful, indicate whether the abstract or full text was inspected.

Flag material uncertainty in individual fields. Omit inapplicable volume, issue and page fields.

## Search Methodology

End every result, including an empty bibliography, with this heading in the conversation language. Report the depth, the international and regional sources actually queried in order, and whether each was searched directly or through a third-party index or domain-scoped web query. Identify accepted publication languages, actual query languages, principal terms and meaningful historical or geographic variants. Briefly describe filtering, ranking, deduplication and verification, distinguishing publisher metadata, DOI records, abstracts and full text. State failed or blocked sources, feasible alternatives used, and coverage gaps. Keep Quick concise. For Deep, include the citation exploration and coverage checks that actually occurred.
